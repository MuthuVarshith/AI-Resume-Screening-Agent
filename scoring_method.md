# Scoring Methodology

The Resume Screening Agent utilizes a transparent, multi-signal scoring system to evaluate candidates. Instead of relying on a single "black box" AI score, it decomposes candidate quality into four distinct signals.

## Multi-Signal Approach Overview

The final candidate ranking is based on a `composite_score` (0.0 to 100.0) generated by a weighted average of four components:
1. Semantic Similarity (40%)
2. Skill Match (30%)
3. Experience (15%)
4. Education (15%)

These weights are configurable in `config.py` but default to the above.

---

## 1. Semantic Similarity (40%)
Evaluates the holistic alignment between the candidate's entire resume text and the job description.

**Formula**:
```text
Score = Cosine_Similarity( Embed(Resume_Text), Embed(JD_Text) ) * 100
```
* **Model**: `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` (Sentence Transformers).
* **Rationale**: Captures implicit matching that keyword systems miss (e.g., matching "built resilient microservices" with "backend distributed systems engineering").

## 2. Skill Match (30%)
Measures how many of the required skills the candidate possesses.

**Formula**:
```text
Score = (Matched_Required_Skills / Total_Required_Skills) * 100
```
### Fuzzy Skill Matching
We do not rely solely on exact string matching. If a required skill is not found exactly, we use embedding similarity.
* If `Cosine_Similarity(Embed(Required_Skill), Embed(Candidate_Skill)) > 0.7`, it counts as a match.
* **Rationale**: Prevents penalizing candidates who write "React.js" instead of "React" or "Node" instead of "NodeJS".

## 3. Experience (15%)
Quantifies the candidate's total professional experience against the minimum required by the role.

**Formula**:
```text
Score = Min(100, (Candidate_Years / JD_Required_Years) * 100)
```
* **Rationale**: If a job requires 5 years of experience, a candidate with 3 years gets a 60%. A candidate with 10 years caps out at 100% to prevent over-indexing on extreme tenure.

## 4. Education (15%)
Compares the candidate's highest attained degree against the job's minimum requirement.

**Formula**:
Uses a hierarchical mapping (e.g., None = 0, High School = 1, Bachelor's = 2, Master's = 3, PhD = 4).
* If Candidate_Level >= JD_Level: `100`
* If Candidate_Level < JD_Level: `(Candidate_Level / JD_Level) * 100`
* **Rationale**: Rewards exceeding or meeting criteria, while proportionally scoring those slightly below.

---

## Composite Score Calculation
The final score is simply the dot product of the scores and their weights:
```text
Composite = (Semantic * 0.40) + (Skills * 0.30) + (Experience * 0.15) + (Education * 0.15)
```

## Edge Cases
* **0 Years Required**: If the JD requires 0 years of experience, anyone gets 100% on the experience module.
* **No Education Required**: If unspecified, the education score evaluates to 100% by default to avoid penalizing self-taught candidates.
* **Empty Resume**: Results in 0 across the board, caught early by the parser.

## What Could Be Improved
* **Recency Bias Weighting**: Weighting skills actively used in the last 2 years higher than skills used 10 years ago.
* **Preferred Skills Bonus**: Currently, preferred skills do not formally factor into the base percentage but could act as a multiplier (e.g., up to 105% cap).
